## Motion and Premium Reference Research

For motion design, motion graphics, and any site or app that animates: find real premium references on the live web before designing. Do not rely on memory of what premium looks like.

### What premium means (judge every reference on these, 1-10 per trait)
- Restraint: few elements, strong hierarchy, one idea per scene. Nothing without a reason.
- Typography: confident type, deliberate pairing, tight tracking and leading, strong scale contrast, no default fonts.
- Space and grid: generous, consistent rhythm, strict alignment, clear margins and safe areas.
- Colour: a small palette built on 60-30-10 with one accent, high contrast, one consistent light.
- Motion language: custom easing curves or springs, never linear, no jumps; consistent durations; staging and overlap; motion explains the layout; reveals tied to meaning.
- Craft: sharp edges and hairlines, texture or grain only on purpose, optical alignment, exact detail.
- Sound (video): designed sound and a clean mix, no filler.
- Performance: smooth at 120 Hz, no layout shifts, fast load.
- Tells of cheap work, reject these: stock or default gradients and glows, coloured side-bar cards, overlapping text, flat single-layer scenes, static or flat camera (scale-and-pan, no parallax or perspective), crossfades, everything entering at once, bouncy default presets, everything centred, template layouts, effects as filler, mixed motion styles, bounce on UI, floating UI fragments instead of the real screen, decorative lines or shapes in the background, no clear focus, the same background in every section, a busy foreground on a busy background (or bare on bare), sections of equal length, a full stop between sections, colours that never leave the repo palette.

### Where to look (verify each one loads; add new ones as found)
Awwwards, Godly, Land-book, Siteinspire, Httpster, Minimal Gallery, Dribbble, Behance, Mobbin (UI flows, may need a login), Motionographer, Art of the Title (title design), Vimeo Staff Picks, plus the sources already listed under References and inspiration. Search the web for current work by style, genre and job, and follow links through to the real studio or site, not just the gallery thumbnail.

### How to research
- Use web search to find candidates, then visit each real site in the browser. Scroll it fully, trigger its interactions, and take screenshots of each section. Depth in tiers to control cost: the 3-5 best references get a full visit (scroll, interactions, screenshots of each section, measured motion). The rest get a quick pass (one screenshot and notes). Full project: 10-20 total. Small: 5-8. Fewer understood beats many skimmed.
- Measure motion where it can be measured, not guessed. In the browser, read the animation values the page sends (CSS transitions and keyframes, animation libraries such as GSAP or Framer Motion, scroll-trigger settings): durations, delays, easing curves, and which properties move. Record them as observed values. If a page's motion cannot be measured, mark it unknown.
- For each reference, write what makes it premium: the specific traits above, where they appear, and the observed values. Not "looks clean".
- Extract the recurring patterns into a short style and motion note: feel, speed range, curves, amount. This becomes the project's motion profile, signed off at Gate 1.
- Log every visited site in `reference-log.md` with the same status rules as Reference Intake.
- Study, never copy. Recreate the traits in the project's own design.
- If the browser is unavailable or a site blocks it, say so and ask the user for another route. Do not work around it.

## Style Profile: Minimal Editorial Short-Form

An optional style to propose when a talking-head Reel or Short fits, never a default. Full guide: `~/.claude/skills/tsg-tweaks/styles/minimal-editorial-shortform.md`. Its values are the reference's own; confirm them on one sample scene first.

- Flat white or near-black backgrounds, one accent colour, paper and paint textures applied lightly, thin tracking lines, illustration or paintings instead of stock footage.
- Captions: short phrases, the one or two key words large and bold at the centre, supporting words small in the gaps.
- Pacing: introduce on the speaker's face, visualise on description; never animate 100% of the runtime; close with a line-and-text scene and a quote scene.
- Workflow: study about 20 references frame by frame, plan every scene in a script document the user approves, then build, revise the first draft, and add sound last (bed, text clicks, line strokes, whooshes).
- Conflicts to resolve: the reference uses an italic serif and Inter; with the no-italics and no-common-defaults rules, propose an upright serif accent and a less common sans on a specimen sheet. Never use a real person's footage, likeness or name for a project without permission.
